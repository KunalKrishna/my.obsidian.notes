> **Purpose:** This guide defines the structure, tone, rules, and quality checklist that every generated cover letter MUST follow.
> An LLM generating a cover letter should treat every section below as a binding instruction set.

---
## 1. What a Cover Letter Is

A cover letter introduces the applicant and briefly explains their qualifications to a prospective employer.
Its purpose is to arouse the employer's interest and secure their attention to the accompanying resume.

**Key principles:**
- The cover letter **highlights** resume content — it does NOT duplicate it word for word.
- It should be a **persuasive** document, not a summary.
- A cover letter should **always accompany a resume** unless the employer explicitly states otherwise. 
---
## 2. Core Writing Rules

These rules are **non-negotiable** and must be applied to every cover letter.
1. **Length:** 3–5 paragraphs. One page maximum.
2. **Always accompany a resume** unless instructed otherwise by the employer.
3. **Focus on what you offer**, not what the employer can do for you. Describe specific interest in the company in the first paragraph; use the body to argue why you are the strongest fit.
4. **Electronic submissions:** Embed the cover letter in the email body; attach the resume separately (unless instructed otherwise).
5. **Salutation:** Address by name and title whenever possible. If no name is available, use "Dear Hiring Manager:" or "Dear [Job Title] Search Committee:" — never "To Whom It May Concern."
6. **One letter per job.** Generic letters signal disinterest and risk name/company mix-ups (an automatic disqualifier).
7. **Sentence variety:** Do NOT begin every sentence or paragraph with the word "I."
8. **Opening hook:** Start with a strong, specific statement that creates a bridge between you and the employer — reference how you learned of the role or a genuine connection to the organization.
9. **Body substance:** Cite brief, concrete facts from your background (education, experience, accomplishments) that complement the target role. Quantify achievements wherever possible.
10. **Closing:** Always thank the employer for their time and consideration in the final paragraph.
11. **Sign-off:** Use "Sincerely," followed by your typed name. An electronic signature image is not required.
12. **Proofread:** Zero spelling or punctuation errors. Any misspelling is an automatic disqualifier.
---
## 3. Reuired Four-Paragraph Structure

Every cover letter MUST follow this four-paragraph structure (AIFF framework):
### Paragraph 1 — ATTENTION (Opening Hook)

**Goal:** State why you are writing, the specific position you are applying for, and how you learned of the opening.
- Reference any prior contact with the employer or a mutual connection at the organization.
- State your educational background or current role and link it to the company's mission.
- Demonstrate that your **professional values align with the company's values**.
- Explain why you want to work in *this* role at *this* organization specifically.

> **Rule:** Do NOT open with "I am excited to apply" or any similar generic phrase. Start with a specific, memorable statement.

--- 
### Paragraph 2 — INTEREST (Why This Company)

**Goal:** Demonstrate genuine, researched interest in the organization — not just the job title.
- Show you understand what makes this company unique or how it stands out from competitors.
- Reference a specific product, service, initiative, mission statement, or company achievement.
- Make clear you are interested in more than a paycheck — connect your personal or professional values to the company's work.

> **Note:** This is the most commonly omitted paragraph. Its absence signals a generic, low-effort application.

---
### Paragraph 3 — FIT (Why You)

**Goal:** Make the persuasive case that you are the strongest candidate for this specific role.
- Refer directly to the job posting requirements and explain how you meet them.
- Use specific examples from past work, internships, academic projects, or leadership roles.
- Highlight the **3 C's:** Communication, Collaboration, and Critical Thinking.
- Showcase interpersonal skills and your ability to work with clients, customers, or colleagues across different settings.
- Do NOT repeat resume bullets verbatim — reframe them as narrative evidence of your fit.
- For recent graduates: explain how your academic background qualifies you. For experienced candidates: cite specific achievements and unique qualifications.  

---
### Paragraph 4 — FOLLOW-UP (Closing)

**Goal:** Thank the employer, reiterate your value, and request an interview. 
- Express strong interest in an interview or next conversation.
- Briefly reiterate your top strength or the core reason you are the best fit.
- Thank the employer sincerely for their time and consideration.
- Include your contact information or reference that it is on the attached resume.

```
[Applicant Full Name]
[Street Address]
[City, State, ZIP Code]
[Email Address] | [Phone Number]

[Date]

[Hiring Manager Name] (or "Hiring Manager" if unknown)

[Title]
[Company Name]
[Street Address]
[City, State, ZIP Code]

Dear [Mr./Ms./Dr. Last Name]: (or "Dear Hiring Manager:")

[Paragraph 1 — ATTENTION]
[Paragraph 2 — INTEREST]
[Paragraph 3 — FIT]
[Paragraph 4 — FOLLOW-UP]

  
Sincerely,
[Typed Full Name]
```

```
[INTEREST / FIT] Through my experience with Kaleidoscope, our campus' first women's fashion magazine, I have had the opportunity to collaborate on topics ranging from HPV vaccination to exam-week beauty tips. As a regular columnist for three semesters, I wrote on the evolution and cultural impact of iconic women's clothing items. My magazine writing seminar added a further dimension: working with a staff of six to build from scratch a women's divorce magazine,

[FIT] My love for the fashion industry drove me to study abroad in Florence, Italy, where I submitted a feature article, "My Italian Life," to Carolina PASSPORT. When my piece was selected as one of ten and the sole Italy submission, I joined the copy-editing staff — a role that sharpened my editorial judgment under real publication deadlines.

[FOLLOW-UP] My communication and research skills align directly with what Redbook seeks in an editorial contributor. My passion for your magazine's mission made applying here an easy decision, and I would welcome the opportunity to prove that in person. Thank you for your time; I look forward to hearing from you.

Sincerely,
[Applicant Name]
```

## 6. Quality Checklist
Use this checklist to validate every cover letter before finalizing it.

### 6.1 Appearance & Format
- [ ] One page in length; business letter formatting
- [ ] Written for a **specific** position at a **specific** company (not generic)
- [ ] Professional language; zero grammatical or spelling errors
- [ ] Standard font (Times New Roman, Arial, or Palatino; 10–12 pt.); matches resume style

### 6.2 Contact Information
- [ ] Applicant contact information appears first, followed by the date
- [ ] Company address appears below the date and above the letter body
- [ ] Salutation names the appropriate contact and ends with a colon (e.g., `Dear Mr. James Smith:`)
- [ ] If no name is available, addressed to `Dear Hiring Manager:` or `Dear Search Committee:` — never "To Whom It May Concern"

### 6.3 Introduction Paragraph (ATTENTION)
- [ ] States the reason for applying and how the applicant learned of the opening
- [ ] References the specific job title (and position number if listed on the posting)
- [ ] Summarizes the strongest qualifications, emphasizing relevance to this role
- [ ] Demonstrates genuine interest in the company and the position

### 6.4 Body Paragraphs (INTEREST + FIT)
- [ ] Clearly shows knowledge of the company and the role
- [ ] Conveys authentic enthusiasm — not just job-seeking language
- [ ] Avoids repetitive phrasing; sentences do NOT all begin with "I" or "My"
- [ ] Uses specific examples from work, volunteer, or education history to demonstrate qualification
- [ ] Employs action-oriented verbs; quantifies accomplishments where possible
- [ ] Focuses on what the candidate's experience can do FOR the organization
- [ ] Does NOT restate the resume bullet-for-bullet — examples are reframed as narrative evidence

### 6.5 Conclusion Paragraph (FOLLOW-UP)
- [ ] Expresses clear interest in an interview
- [ ] Reiterates top strength and why the candidate is the strongest fit
- [ ] Includes or references contact information for the employer
- [ ] Thanks the recipient for their time and consideration

### 6.6 Closing
- [ ] Signs off with a professional closing (`Sincerely,`)
- [ ] Full name typed below the closing line
